\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Design.}
Eight policies (Table~\ref{tab:arms}) span three views: the head camera, a wrist fisheye and a fixed external camera. Teleoperated actions come from the encoders except in arm 6, and glove labels from the head camera unless stated. The teleoperator watched the head-camera preview and all three cameras recorded every episode, so the arms share demonstration trajectories and differ only in observation and data composition. Arms 1--4 test co-training at two teleoperation budgets (172 and 48 episodes); arm 6 tests head-camera labels in place of encoders; arms 5, 7 and 8 test the view: arm 5 is the wrist-camera UMI recipe \citep{chi2024umi} with a fisheye added to the glove for this ablation only, arm 7 a fixed camera (\SI{10}{\percent} fewer glove frames to body occlusion), and arm 8 head labels on the wrist view, separating the view from the labels.

\paragraph{Label accuracy.}
With the ball on the YAM and the encoders as reference, the chain places the tool point within \SI{6.5}{mm} median (\SI{14.1}{mm} p90) and \SI{1.55}{\degree} (\SI{2.70}{\degree}) over 5\,065 frames. The jaw width, checked on 112 teleoperated episodes (24.6k frames), is within \SI{3.5}{\percent} of stroke at the median (\SI{7.9}{\percent} p90), \SI{2.3}{\percent} in the closed state that decides a grasp, with no lag and a label on every frame in 9 of 10 episodes; the error is flat over workspace, distance, elevation and viewing angle (\SI{2.8}{}--\SI{4.8}{\percent} median per bin; App.~\ref{app:width}). A policy trained on these labels instead of encoders (arm 6 against arm 1), scored on the same encoder-labelled held-out episodes, loses \SI{3}{\percent} in validation loss (0.328 against 0.318) and is within noise on the robot (15/25 against 18/25; 5 placements won to 8 lost, exact McNemar $p = 0.58$).

\paragraph{Co-training and the teleoperation budget.}
Co-training across sources has precedent \citep{fu2024mobilealoha,kareer2025egomimic}. Adding the 194 glove segments to 172 teleoperated episodes lowers the robot-validation loss from 0.318 to 0.263 (arm 2 against 1); added to 48 episodes, from 0.464 to 0.295 (arm 4 against 3). By log-linear interpolation of the teleop-only curve (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}) the glove set is worth 65--100 teleoperated episodes, 2--3 glove demonstrations per teleoperated one, and the 48-episode mix lands at the level of 96--172 teleoperated episodes; the gain shrinks with budget (0.075 at 24 episodes, 0.011 at 96), so glove data stretches a small teleoperated set rather than replacing the last few dozen. On the robot the 48-episode mix succeeds in 17/25 against 10/25 for 48 teleoperated episodes alone (9 placements won to 2 lost, $p = 0.065$) and 18/25 for all 172 (4 to 5). Every failure of both 48-episode arms is a missed grasp; the difference is recovery: in scene 5 the mix missed, backed off and re-grasped where the teleop-only policy never closed in.

\paragraph{Which camera.}
Offline the four co-trained views (arms 2, 5, 7, 8) sit within 0.011 of each other (0.252--0.263), and offline loss is a weak proxy for rollouts \citep{mandlekar2021robomimic}. The rollouts separate them: head camera 20/25, wrist fisheye 15/25, fixed camera 12/25, head labels on the wrist view 14/25. On shared placements the head camera wins 6/1, 11/3 and 8/2 against them ($p = 0.13$, $0.06$, $0.11$), so no pair clears $0.05$ at 25 placements, but no arm beats arm 2 on more than three. The failure modes split by view: head-camera policies fail by never grasping, wrist-camera policies by dropping the block mid-carry (12 of the 21 failures of arms 5 and 8, against 1 of 45 for the head-camera arms, a slip at lift-off). In the operator's notes every wrist-view drop came while the fisheye looked away from the bin and none with the bin in view (Fig.~\ref{fig:rollouts}); the head camera keeps gripper and bin in one frame through the carry. Same view, other labels (arm 8 against 5): 14/25 against 15/25, indistinguishable. Same labels, other view (8 against 2): 20/25 falls to 14/25 and the no-grasps become drops. The fixed camera (arm 7) is the weakest and fails like the head view, by not grasping (12 of 13); it was mounted low because the positioner took the high vantage point, so its \SI{10}{\percent} occlusion is an upper bound. Median time to success is \SI{16}{}--\SI{19}{s} for every arm: the views differ in whether the task completes, not in how fast.
