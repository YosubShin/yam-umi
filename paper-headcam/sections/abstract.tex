\begin{abstract}
Handheld demonstration devices decouple robot data collection from physical hardware, but conventional designs burden the hand with wrist cameras, cables, and complex tracking systems like SLAM or IMUs, while fixed external cameras suffer from severe body occlusion. We show that a single egocentric camera (\$122) worn on the collector's head can replace both the wrist camera and the pose tracker. By tracking printed fiducials on an electronics-free glove (\$22) relative to paper tiles taped around the workspace, the head camera extracts 6-DoF gripper pose and jaw width. The head naturally tracks the hand without torso occlusion, preserving visual context of both the gripper and task targets. At deployment, the same camera is mounted on a low-cost (\$120) robot-adjacent arm that mimics the collector's viewpoint from the robot's joint state. Because collection does not occupy the robot, operators can work in parallel, recording 2.0--3.1 demonstrations per minute compared to 1.6 by teleoperation. Benchmarked against robot encoders, head-camera labels achieve \SI{6.5}{mm} median tool-point error and \SI{1.6}{\degree} rotation error, maintaining \SI{93}{}--\SI{100}{\percent} frame coverage per session (versus \SI{78}{}--\SI{92}{\percent} for an occluded fixed camera). Downstream imitation policies trained on these labels incur only a \SI{3}{\percent} validation loss penalty compared to encoder ground truth. In co-training, adding 194 glove demonstrations to 172 teleoperated episodes reduces ACT validation loss from 0.318 to 0.263; added to 48 teleoperated episodes, it cuts loss from 0.464 to 0.295, matching the performance of 96--172 teleoperated demonstrations alone. On the physical robot, the 48-episode co-trained policy succeeds in 17/25 trials (versus 10/25 for 48 teleoperation episodes alone and 18/25 for the full 172), and the full co-trained head-camera policy succeeds in 20/25, outperforming conventional wrist-camera policies (15/25) that drop objects when task goals leave their narrow view, and fixed-camera baselines (12/25).
\end{abstract}
