\section{The idea}
\label{sec:idea}
Every UMI-style device answers two questions: what does the policy see, and where was the gripper. The standard answer to the first is a camera on the wrist, which by construction sees what the robot's wrist camera will see. The standard answer to the second is a pose tracker on the same wrist, and every tracker has a price. SLAM over the wrist camera \citep{chi2024umi} needs a mapping pass and a textured scene, and an episode is lost when tracking is \todo{cite}; an IMU drifts \todo{cite}; a VR controller adds weight to the hand and brings a tracking system of its own \citep{vrtracking2024}; a fixed 4K camera over the workspace, the choice of our earlier glove \citep{yamumi2026}, gives absolute labels but ties collection to a tripod and loses the hand behind the collector's own body, \SI{8}{}--\SI{22}{\percent} of frames per session in the data below (\S\ref{sec:labels}). The wrist camera has prices of its own. It puts electronics on the glove: a camera, a cable and a stream to synchronise with the tracker. And it frames the jaws and little else, so the goal of a carry, here the bin, can leave the policy's view while the task still depends on it.

Two of the open problems this workshop names are in-the-wild tracking that does not rest on SLAM, and the viewpoint gap between what a handheld device records and what the robot sees. Our answer to both is one camera, worn. The collector's head follows the hand by habit, keeps the gripper roughly centred at arm's length and is never occluded by the torso; from its view the fiducial ball gives the pose, the jaw tags give the width, and printed AprilTag tiles taped on the table give the world frame, so the glove itself carries no electronics at all, and the scene around the gripper, bin included, stays in frame. The same camera is the policy's observation. On the robot a small servo-driven printed arm carries it and imitates the head from the robot's joint state alone, identically during teleoperation and rollouts, so training and deployment share one viewpoint distribution. The price is a moving camera at deployment; the claim is that this price buys the wrist camera, the tracker and the cable, and that the policy is as good. A fixed camera was kept only as an ablation.
