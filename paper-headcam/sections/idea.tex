\section{The idea}
\label{sec:idea}
Teleoperation occupies a robot and an operator for every demonstration. Handheld devices decouple collection from the robot: with ours a collector records 2.0--3.1 demonstrations per minute against 1.6 by teleoperation on the same task (\S\ref{sec:system}), in parallel and without robot time, with a \$22 printed glove, a \$122 wearable camera and four paper tiles. The glove is purely mechanical, a replica of the robot's gripper carrying fiducials and nothing else, and the one head-worn camera is at once the policy's observation and the source of the gripper's pose and jaw width, so there is no second sensor stream to synchronise or calibrate against.

Every UMI-style device answers two questions: \emph{what does the policy see}, and \emph{how is the gripper tracked}? The conventional answer puts both on the wrist. SLAM on the wrist camera \citep{chi2024umi,campos2021orbslam3} needs a mapping pass and textured scenes and loses track under occlusion or fast motion; a visual-inertial sensor adds a second device \citep{fastumi2024}; a VR controller adds weight (\SI{119}{g} of YUBI's \SI{319}{g} \citep{yubi2026,tinyumi2026}) and external tracking infrastructure \citep{vrtracking2024,quest2rehab2022}; a fixed overhead camera \citep{yamumi2026} avoids drift but ties collection to a mount and loses the hand behind the body in \SI{8}{}--\SI{22}{\percent} of frames (\S\ref{sec:labels}). Each tracker is a second stream beside the camera, with its own clock and calibration to reconcile, and the wrist camera itself adds cable and weight while its view is tied to the hand: even through UMI's \SI{155}{\degree} fisheye the frame holds what the jaws face, so a drop-off bin leaves it once the hand lifts or turns \citep{hsu2022hands}.

We answer both with one head-worn camera, addressing two challenges this workshop names \citep{umiarena2026}: tracking without SLAM and the human-to-robot viewpoint gap. Gaze precedes the hand \citep{land2001eye}, so the head keeps the gripper at arm's length, unoccluded by the torso, with the workspace around it. From that vantage an 11-face fiducial ball gives the pose, tags on the jaws the opening, and four AprilTag tiles \citep{olson2011apriltag} the world frame. At deployment the camera rides a small printed servo arm beside the robot that imitates head motion from the robot's joint state, identically in teleoperation and rollouts, so glove, teleoperated and rollout data share one viewpoint. EgoMimic \citep{kareer2025egomimic} co-trains on egocentric human video with the hand pose estimated from the video; our collector holds a replica of the robot's gripper, so contact and jaw width are the robot's. Active-vision teleoperation \citep{chuang2025avaloha,cheng2024opentelevision} steers a robot camera from a VR headset; our positioner needs neither operator nor headset.
