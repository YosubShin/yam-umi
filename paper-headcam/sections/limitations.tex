\section{Limitations and what is next}
\label{sec:discussion}
The tile layout is refined by median single-tile poses rather than a full bundle adjustment \citep{triggs2000bundle}, which should tighten it further. A label needs a tile in view as well as the glove, so coverage depends on where the tiles are placed; we followed the placement rules of \S\ref{sec:system} but tried only one layout. The positioner lags \SI{0.4}{s} and hits joint limits on wide sweeps; its viewpoint model is a linear fit on one session with no tuning against rollouts, and the jaws were often occluded by the robot's wrist-camera bracket, which the head-camera system does not need and which stayed on only for the wrist-view ablations. Removing it is the cheapest next gain. The head camera sees the collector's forearm and fingers, so glove-only policies do not transfer (validation 0.52 against 0.26 co-trained), as with the fixed-camera device. Controls not run: a higher fixed camera; teleoperation under a wrist-camera preview, since the operator watched the head view; a second seed, so the paired tests sit at $p \geq 0.06$ with a consistent ranking; and a second collector, as one person with one glove recorded every session. A workspace away from the robot needs no robot-side calibration: the tile-to-base link, fitted here with the robot holding the ball, can be left free per session as UMI-style pipelines do with relative trajectories; the collector only needs tiles taped down with one, preferably two, in view. Nothing is single-handed: a second glove with its own tag ids is read by the same camera, and one positioner serves both arms. Hardware \citep{yamumi2026} and tools \citep{forwardcamumi2026} are MIT-licensed; the robot-side calibration and dataset tools are not yet released. Next are the bimanual version, multi-tile workspaces, in-the-wild sessions and a second task.
