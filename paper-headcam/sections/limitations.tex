\section{Limitations and what is next}
\label{sec:discussion}
The tile layout is refit from one session by median single-tile poses rather than bundle-adjusted \citep{triggs2000bundle}; a proper adjustment should bring the single-tile jitter below the current \SI{4}{}--\SI{9}{mm}. The positioner lags the gripper by \SI{0.4}{s} and hits joint limits on the largest sweeps; a lighter camera or a faster arm would close that. The head camera also sees the collector's arm and the glove's fingers, which is the appearance gap that keeps glove-only policies from transferring (robot-validation loss 0.52 against 0.26 co-trained), as in the fixed-camera version of the device. The fixed-camera ablation was mounted low because the positioner took the high vantage point; a higher fixed camera would occlude less, though it cannot follow the hand. Parallel collection was not tested: one collector and one glove produced all four sessions \todo{confirm}. A workspace away from the robot still needs a tile-to-base link like the one fitted here with the robot carrying the ball \todo{check how glove data from a new place maps into the robot frame}. Hardware and software are open \citep{yamumi2026,forwardcamumi2026}. \todo{what is next: multi-tile workspaces, in-the-wild sessions, a second task}
